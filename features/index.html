A story from Chad

A Translator and the Angry Imam

Ten million people in Chad still haven't heard the gospel. This translation team travels many dangerous miles to check each new translation with the communities who will use it — this time, with the village imam watching.

Outside, teenagers waited for a signal to stone the visitors. But as the stories were read aloud, the anger in the room softened. By the end, the imam himself stood up: “If anyone says Jesus is not the savior, he is not a real Muslim, and he should be stoned.”

“I bless the name of the Lord for unfoldingWord and Open Bible Stories. This instrument is inspired! We are sowing seeds in the hearts of Muslims that are like bombs, and one day they will burst into life.”
— A Bible translation partner, Chad

This kind of story isn't unique. Across Africa, Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East, churches continue choosing Open Bible Stories because it removes barriers to sharing God's Word.
